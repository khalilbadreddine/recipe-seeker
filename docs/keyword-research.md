# Recipe Seeker — Keyword Research

**Date:** 2026-09-17
**Method:** ~30 transactional + ~13 informational live Google SERP checks (US). Difficulty ratings are honest, based on who actually ranks on page one and how strong those pages are — not on keyword-tool difficulty scores.
**Scope:** US-focused, new domain, 6–12 month horizon. Research only — no site changes made.

**Rating key:** Easy (weak/misaligned page one — very winnable) · Easy–Medium (thin or beatable competition) · Medium (one or two strong pages, but beatable with better content) · Medium–Hard (authorities present but content is old/misaligned) · Hard (EatingWell/Kitchn/health authorities with dedicated, fresh pages — avoid for now).

**Site context:** The site currently has 24 recipes, 12 nutrient hubs, 2 guides, search, and standard pages. The author persona (Emily Carter) is not credentialed — medical/deficiency claims need care and disclaimers. Full nutrition totals are authored estimates; hero ingredients are USDA-checked.

## Top 10 attack-first priorities

These are the keywords to hit first, ordered by winnability × demand × fit with the brand:

1. **iron rich vegetarian dinner recipes** — Easy. Almost no US recipe competition: a Canadian health-provider PDF, an irrelevant Kitchn skillet roundup, a YouTube video, and an Indian recipe site. A dedicated US collection page should win fast. Hub: Iron.
2. **high iron dinner recipes** — Easy–Medium. SERP is government PDFs, a flat-iron steak mismatch, a WebMD slideshow, and an Indian page. No real recipe collection. Hub: Iron.
3. **vitamin C rich dinner recipes** — Easy–Medium. No definitive recipe collection exists: a vegetable explainer article, small recipe sites, PDFs, and scraped/reposted listicles. Hub: Vitamin C.
4. **folate rich dinner recipes** — Easy–Medium. Extremely thin: a 2020 pregnancy-brand recipe, a Florida government PDF, a nutrient-browser tool, a CDC graphic. Hub: Folate.
5. **high iron breakfast ideas** — Easy–Medium. Government PDFs, Nutrabay (Indian e-commerce), SHEfinds. No authoritative US recipe collection. Hub: Iron.
6. **folate rich breakfast recipes** — Easy–Medium. EatingWell's results are generic high-fiber pages, not folate-specific; plus a Florida PDF and BBC Good Food. A folate-specific collection would be the most relevant result. Hub: Folate.
7. **zinc rich vegetarian recipes** — Easy–Medium. PubMed paper, one small blogger recipe, Indian recipe collections, an One Green Planet listicle. Thin US-focused coverage. Hub: Zinc.
8. **calcium rich dinner recipes** — Easy–Medium. WebMD slideshow, old PDFs, a Food.com recipe, a Cleveland Clinic foods list, a USDA/WIC page. Strong recipe-list opening. Hub: Calcium.
9. **30g protein breakfast recipes** — Easy–Medium. Results include genuinely weak/low-authority sites (DiningAndCooking, Newsy Today, Happy Way, Keto Flavor House) — a sign of an under-contested keyword. Hub: Protein.
10. **magnesium rich dinner recipes** — Medium. The #1 result is a solo dietitian's blog (eastewart.com), followed by a WIC page and smaller/older sites. Winnable with better-quantified, genuinely tested recipes. Hub: Magnesium.

**Why these:** the pattern is clear — the nutrients with the *thinnest* SERP competition (iron, folate, vitamin C, zinc, magnesium, calcium dinner) are exactly the site's weakest-coverage hubs (calcium: 7 recipes, vitamin C: 7, magnesium: 5, zinc: 2, potassium: 3). Demand exists; supply of good recipe content doesn't. Meanwhile protein and fiber keywords are dominated by EatingWell/Kitchn and should be attacked only via narrow sub-niches (see below).

### Gaps (keywords with weak or no existing-recipe coverage)

1. **High-iron breakfast** — only Egg Muffin Cups and Overnight Oats fit; need 3–4 more iron-rich breakfast recipes (the 26-recipe batch in progress should cover this).
2. **Zinc dinner** — only Beef & Lentil Chili qualifies; need several more (pumpkin-seed/cashew/chickpea dinners).
3. **Folate breakfast** — Egg Toast + Frittata; needs 1–2 more.
4. **Magnesium dinner** — Roasted Veggie Farro Bowl only; needs 3–4 more.
5. **Potassium** — only Sweet Potato Bowl; hub is thin but keyword is tricky — deprioritize.
6. **30g-protein breakfast** — several recipes may qualify but none advertise precise 30g+ counts; add "protein grams per serving" badges to breakfast recipes.
7. **Vitamin C dinner** — White Bean & Kale Soup, Sweet Potato Bowl; needs 2–3 more citrus/pepper-heavy dinners.
8. **Iron + vitamin C pairings** — no dedicated recipes positioned for the absorption angle; flag recipes that naturally combine both (lentil + lemon, spinach + tomato).

### Cannibalization risks

- **Protein:** ~10 recipes could all plausibly target "high protein X" variants. Rule: each recipe page targets ONE long-tail keyword (e.g. "high protein breakfast without eggs" for the egg-free options); hub/collection pages target the head terms ("30g protein breakfast recipes" collection).
- **Iron:** Lentil Curry, Chickpea Bowl, Quinoa Power Bowl, Chickpea & Spinach Curry all suit "iron rich vegetarian dinner recipes." Consolidate behind ONE collection page listing all four; individual recipes target narrower variants ("iron rich lentil dinner", "iron rich chickpea dinner").
- **Folate:** several recipes span folate + fiber + iron. Same rule: collection page owns the head term; recipe pages own the long tail.
- **Meal-prep/macro keywords** ("high protein meal prep lunch ideas") overlap with any lunch-positioned recipe — keep these keywords off recipe pages for now (hard difficulty, deferred).
- Do not create one blog post per keyword variant; one collection page per (nutrient × meal) pair, then internal-link recipes into it.

- All YMYL/medical-adjacent content needs the site's standard disclaimer and must avoid diagnosis/treatment promises (the author is not a credentialed professional).
